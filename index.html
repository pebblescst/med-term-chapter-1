Abbreviations — simple (ADHD), acronym (AIDS), or dangerous (OU, qid).
Key clinical concepts
Diagnosis
Present condition — what the patient has now
Prognosis
Future course or expected outcome
Acute
Short-term, sharp onset (acute appendicitis)
Chronic
Long-term, recurring (chronic asthma)
Sign — Objective
Observable and measurable by others. Does not require the patient to report it.
e.g. fever (thermometer reading), rash
Symptom — Subjective
Reported by the patient. Cannot be directly observed or measured.
e.g. pain, nausea, dizziness
The CARD method — 4 steps to decode any term
C
Check
Identify all word parts. Break the term into its root(s), prefix, and suffix.
A
Assign
Assign a meaning to each word part using your knowledge of combining forms and suffixes.
R
Reverse
Move the suffix meaning to the front of your translation. Suffixes define the main subject.
D
Define
Combine the reversed meanings into one clear, grammatical definition.
Example: Glossitisworked
C
Check: gloss + -itis
A
Assign: tongue / inflammation of
R
Reverse: inflammation of / tongue
D
Define: Inflammation of the tongue
Example: Gastralgiaworked
C
Check: gastr + -algia
A
Assign: stomach / pain
R
Reverse: pain / the stomach
D
Define: Pain of the stomach
💡 Bonus: Is "chronic gastralgia" a sign or a symptom? It's a symptom — the patient reports the pain; it cannot be directly observed or measured.
5 rules for building medical terms
1
Suffix starts with a vowel → drop the combining vowel
arthr/o + -itis = arthritis
'-itis' starts with 'i' (vowel) → drop the 'o'
2
Suffix starts with a consonant → keep the combining vowel
arthr/o + -plasty = arthroplasty
'-plasty' starts with 'p' (consonant) → keep the 'o'
3
Combining form ends with same vowel suffix begins with → drop one vowel
endocardi/o + -itis = endocarditis (not endocardiitis)
4
Two or more combining forms → keep the combining vowel between them
gastr/o + enter/o + -itis = gastroenteritis
The 'o' is kept even though 'enter' starts with a vowel
5
Multiple combining forms → follow anatomical or logical order
esophag/o + gastr/o + duoden/o + -scopy = esophagogastroduodenoscopy
Follows the anatomical path: esophagus → stomach → duodenum
💡 Memory aid: Rule 4 overrides Rule 1 — when two combining forms are joined, always keep the vowel between them, even if the next form starts with a vowel.

Chapter 1 — Key takeaways
1
~90% of medical terms are decodable using Greek/Latin word parts; ~10% must be memorized
2
Diagnosis = present condition; Prognosis = future condition
3
Acute = short-term / sharp onset; Chronic = long-term / recurring
4
Sign = objective (observable); Symptom = subjective (patient-reported)
5
CARD Method: Check → Assign → Reverse → Define
6
5 building rules govern how combining forms, roots, and suffixes are joined
7
Combining vowel 'o' is kept before consonants; dropped before vowels (with exceptions for multiple combining forms)
8
Pathology suffixes: -algia, -itis, -emia, -megaly, -stenosis, -sclerosis, -ptosis, -malacia
9
Prefixes modify terms for location, quantity, or state (hyper-, hypo-, inter-, intra-…)
10
Singular/plural follow Latin/Greek patterns: -a→-ae, -um→-a, -us→-i, -osis→-oses
